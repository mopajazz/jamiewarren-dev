# Course Opportunity Scan

Use this process to find where a Tiny Causal World would produce more learning than explanation, practice questions, or static media.

## Reconstruct instructional intent

Give course sources different roles rather than treating every file as equivalent:

- **Syllabus and course outcomes:** define level, scope, priorities, sequence constraints, and institutional language.
- **Assignments and rubrics:** reveal what learners must actually decide, diagnose, create, explain, or perform.
- **Canvas modules and pages:** reveal sequence, prerequisite relationships, current activities, repetition, and instructional gaps.
- **Presentations, readings, and handouts:** reveal the instructor's conceptual model, examples, terminology, and emphasis.
- **YouTube links and other media:** use available transcripts or captions to identify demonstrations, explanations, cases, and recurring misconceptions.
- **External sources:** verify causal accuracy and complete missing domain knowledge without displacing intentional course framing.

Do not infer importance from file length, visual polish, or repetition alone. Trace outcomes to assessed performance and then to the instruction meant to support it.

## Identify candidates

Favor concepts that contain several of these properties:

- an invisible process that could become observable;
- variables a learner can manipulate meaningfully;
- delayed, cumulative, competing, or probabilistic consequences;
- a feedback loop, threshold, equilibrium, bottleneck, or tradeoff;
- an authentic decision whose effects can be replayed or compared;
- a predictable misconception that system behavior can challenge;
- a gap between being able to repeat an explanation and being able to reason with it;
- strong alignment with a consequential assignment or outcome.

Do not force a simulation onto straightforward recall, terminology recognition, a fixed procedure already taught well through demonstration, or content lacking meaningful learner agency.

## Rank candidates

Evaluate each serious candidate on:

1. **Instructional leverage:** how much the world could improve consequential performance.
2. **Causal clarity:** whether a coherent relationship can be modeled truthfully.
3. **Learner agency:** whether actions can test ideas rather than merely advance screens.
4. **Feedback value:** whether consequences can make latent state inferable.
5. **Evidence alignment:** strength of the connection to course outcomes, assignments, rubrics, and instruction.
6. **Tiny-world feasibility:** whether one primary causal claim can be isolated without distortion.

Penalize avoidable complexity, weak evidence, decorative game potential, and concepts whose correctness depends on unavailable expertise or data.

## Opportunity Gate output

Present the three strongest candidates in ranked order. For each, state:

- source evidence and course location;
- aligned outcome or performance;
- proposed primary causal claim;
- learner action and observable consequence;
- misconception or reasoning gap addressed;
- why simulation is more useful than a simpler activity;
- major fidelity or implementation risk.

Recommend one candidate decisively. Stop for approval before expanding it into a causal-world model.
